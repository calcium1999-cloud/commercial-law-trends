### ECGI（1 篇）

#### The Corporate Governance Implications of Presidentialism
**作者**：Jeffrey Gordon
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：The U.S. Supreme Court has pushed the United States into a new system of public governance, “Presidentialism,” which gives unprecedented economic regulatory power and enforcement power to the President. Compensation arrangements designed to align shareholder and managerial interests now give the President additional power to obtain corporate compliance with preferred policy positions because of the concentrated firm-specific pecuniary stakes of managers and directors. In the name of protecting the democracy against authoritarian over-reach, we need new corporate governance institutions that insulate decision-makers from this pecuniary pressure, here, a Public Integrity Committee at the board level, compensated without stock and protected through staggered terms. Similarly, the exposure of asset managers to pressure over their stewardship voting strategies call for divestment of voting authority to decentralized but autonomous voting offices. This preserves the efficiency of centralized fund administration and portfolio assembly and marketing while divesting the asset manager of voting authority that makes it a potential target of Presidentialist pressure.
**关键词**：Stewardship; Voting; Political Economy; Company Law; Regulation; Capital; ESG; Institutional Investors; Technology; Control; Litigation; Carbon emission

### OBLB（1 篇）

#### Prediction Markets: Who Regulates a Bet on the Future?
**作者**：Filippo Annunziata, Thomaz de Arruda
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：The rapid growth of prediction markets, and the equally rapid multiplication of disputes concerning their regulation, have revived a question that is far older than the platforms on which such contracts are now traded, namely whether an agreement whose payoff depends upon the occurrence or non-occurrence of a future event is to be regarded as a financial instrument or, rather, as a wager. The issue is not merely taxonomic. Upon its answer depend the identity of the competent authority, the applicability of the rules on market abuse, investor protection and cross-border provision of services, and, ultimately, the legitimacy of an industry whose turnover now runs into billions of dollars. In a recent working paper, Prediction Markets between the United States and the European Union: A Comparative Analysis of the Legal Classification of Event Contracts , available on SSRN , we examine how the two principal regulatory systems of the Western world classify what is, in economic substance, the same product. We seek to show that the divergence between them is at once narrower and more instructive than is commonly assumed. The starting point of the analysis is that the two systems have approached the problem from opposite ends.
