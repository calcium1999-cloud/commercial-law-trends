# 商业法律研究动向

**报告周期**：2026-11-03 — 2026-11-04
**数据来源**：ECGI | Harvard | OBLB | ProMarket | CLS | Yale JREG | Jotwell | BWI | NY Fed | ECB | BoE

## 📊 本周研究趋势概括

本期十一大来源共新增14篇文章，来自Harvard、OBLB、ProMarket、CLS与NY Fed；按主分类计，金融监管7篇、公司治理3篇、反垄断2篇、科技数据与AI及其他各1篇。看似分散的董事薪酬、代币化证券、股东提案、银行处置与产品责任研究，都在追问同一问题：法律如何选择可观察指标、划定制度边界，并据此分配决策权与风险。
公司治理研究把注意力从制度形式转向实际作用。董事薪酬文章显示，股权薪酬占比并不能稳定代表激励强度，真正需要测量的是薪酬对相对股东回报的敏感度；尽责管理研究同样区分会议、投票等“活动”与可归因的治理影响。特拉华州修法反弹和规则14a-8争议则说明，法院或监管者若忽视控股股东、州法与公司自治的政治能力，程序性权力配置可能反过来重塑实体治理规则。
金融监管文章集中呈现基础设施与责任规则的同步重构。代币化证券可能缩短结算并降低中介层级，却也把股东名册、投票、持续交易、网络安全和发行人选择权重新组合；Form S-8与第701条的新解释通过技术性登记规则降低股权激励交易成本；PSLRA风险披露的巡回法院分歧则表明，同一警示文本是否承担责任，取决于法院如何区分未来风险与已经实现的事实。
反垄断与合同法的两篇文章共同强调法律后果不能脱离归责链条。Livronsa案拒绝把基准卡特尔的无效当然扩张至未参与卡特尔的抵押合同，而并购效率抗辩研究则以“重大问题原则”和成文法文本质疑法院自行创设抗辩。二者都要求在竞争损害、行为人参与和法定授权之间建立可证明的连接，而不是只凭政策目的扩张责任或例外。
监管能力本身也是本期的研究对象。跨国实证研究发现，受任期限制的领导人在金融危机后更可能推出有利于银行和自身职业利益的改革；纽约联储研究则指出，银行监管不仅被动读取账面资本，还会通过要求确认损失主动生产用于关闭决策的“硬信息”。SEC主席关于资本形成、数字资产和州公司法的议程进一步显示，所谓回归基本原则实质上仍是在投资者保护、创新与监管权限之间作制度选择。
比较法与商法一般理论为这些具体争议提供了更宽的框架。中英欧对AI能否构成“产品”的不同处理，揭示责任边界取决于法律是否把软件、云服务与实体商品视为同一风险载体；统一商法理论则试图让商法、金融法、破产法、证券法和组织法共享对信息不对称、集体行动与私人秩序失灵的分析。方法上，本期既有大样本和跨国实证，也有规范与教义论证；阅读结论时应分别检验测量、因果识别与法定文本依据。

### OBLB（2 篇）

#### A Rigged Benchmark, an Invalid Contract? The Attribution Problem after Livronsa
**作者**：Ernesto Suárez Puga
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：A rigged benchmark does not necessarily mean an invalid contract. In Case C-60/25 Livronsa (2026) , the Court of Justice of the European Union rejected the automatic extension of antitrust nullity to a mortgage term referencing Euribor. The argument developed here is a separate contractual one: a contracting party involved in producing a benchmark implicitly undertakes, as a matter of good faith, not to manipulate it. That undertaking concerns its own conduct. It does not make it the guarantor of every other contributor's honesty. In Livronsa , the Court considered an Italian mortgage whose interest rate referenced Euribor. The borrower invoked the European Commission’s Euro Interest Rate Derivatives cartel decisions to challenge the interest term. The lender had not participated in the cartel, and the loan neither formed part of nor implemented the prohibited arrangement. The Court held that the automatic nullity under Article 101(2) of the Treaty on the Functioning of the European Union did not extend to that term: the parties were outside the cartel, and their contract concerned a different market (paragraphs 35–38). The Commission decisions bound national courts only within the scope of the infringement established. The finding of a restriction by object did not require proof of actual effects and did not, by itself, establish specific effects on Euribor’s level (paragraphs 42–45).

**主题**：反垄断, 金融监管

#### When is AI a product? China’s new approach and the choices facing the UK Law Commission
**作者**：Mohammad Samiul Huq
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：In September 2026, China’s Supreme People’s Court (SPC) issued new judicial guidance on disputes involving artificial intelligence. Among the issues addressed is a fundamental question for product liability: when should AI itself be treated as a product? China has drawn a relatively clear boundary, keeping product liability connected to AI embodied in physical products rather than standalone AI services. The development is particularly timely for the UK, where the Law Commission is reviewing the UK product liability regime under the Consumer Protection Act 1987 (CPA) , including its treatment of digital products and emerging technologies. The EU has already taken a different route by expressly bringing software within its revised product liability regime. These contrasting approaches provide a useful lens through which to consider the choices now facing the UK Law Commission. Section 1(2) of the CPA defines a product as ‘any goods or electricity’. AI contained in a physical product presents less difficulty than non-physical products. If an AI-enabled refrigerator is defective and catches fire, the refrigerator is a physical product and product liability can operate in the ordinary way. The harder question is standalone AI supplied as software or through the cloud. The EU has answered broadly. Article 4(1) of Directive (EU) 2024/2853 expressly includes software within the definition of a product.
