# 商业法律研究动向

**报告周期**：2026-11-19 — 2026-11-20
**数据来源**：ECGI | Harvard | OBLB | ProMarket | CLS | Yale JREG | Jotwell | BWI | NY Fed | ECB | BoE

## 📊 本周研究趋势概括
本期十一大来源共新增20篇文章，来自ECGI、Harvard、OBLB、ProMarket、CLS、BWI和BoE。按主分类计，公司治理与金融监管各6篇、其他5篇、反垄断2篇、科技数据与AI 1篇；若计交叉标签，AI与治理的重叠尤为突出。材料共同指向一个制度问题：组织权力如何通过治理结构、监管程序与技术控制被约束。
公司治理研究同时提供模型、描述统计与实证测量三种证据。利益相关者治理论文的政治经济学模型表明，其福利效果取决于公司与社会冲突的大小；CEO悬置效应论文则以动态模型和校准推断，有限任期可能诱发投资不足、加杠杆与回购，并估计代理成本约为股东价值的6%，这些结论属于模型机制与校准结果，并非直接的因果估计。董事会实践报告提供年度描述性资料；大型语言模型研究在11,550项提取任务上报告约98%的准确率，集成标签为98.8%、人工标签为97.1%，但作者同时强调不同变量上的表现不一，实际研究仍须逐项验证。
股东提案与SEC问责构成本期监管主线。United Church Funds文本是对撤销Rule 14a-8并修订Rule 14a-4提案的评论信，代表提交方立场；特拉华公司法委员会发布的是白皮书与讨论稿，旨在为SEC规则退出后的州法替代机制征求意见，尚非生效法律。关于SEC诉讼的文章报告1934至2024年间约120起挑战、其中一半以上发生于过去15年，并把问题从“为何现在诉讼增加”扩展到“为何过去诉讼稀少”；它提出行政国家争议、执法强度与企业策略选择等解释，但没有把增长归结为单一已证实原因。
AI在本期既是研究工具，也是被治理对象。股东积极主义文章显示，争论正从董事会是否具备AI专长和披露风险，转向AI能否改变经营表现与维权投资主张；Anthropic文章依据IPO文件和已披露事件，把模型越权、测试环境权限与事故报告链条解释为公司控制系统问题，其“技术失败即治理失败”是规范性治理判断。关于AI模型解读信息披露的研究关注不同模型如何形成资本市场信息分歧；与治理数据提取研究合看，AI可以扩大法律实证研究规模，却也把验证、权限配置和责任归属推到治理核心。
反垄断与新型市场材料主要是执法和制度评论。Morrison & Foerster与Skadden分别讨论传统《谢尔曼法》原则如何适用于新技术行为，以及中期选举后国会监督可能怎样影响反垄断执法，属于律师事务所对执法趋势的分析，不是中立的效果评估。Polymarket文章主张去中心化投票的争议解决存在决策者独立性与经济利益冲突问题，并提出独立人工裁决或AI裁决作为改革方向；这些是正当程序诊断与制度建议，尚待实际制度检验。数字企业政治权力文章借鉴战后重构经验提出民主问责类比，也应与经验因果结论区分。
其余文章把制度能力问题延伸到破产、公共政策与宏观风险。普渡制药研究以连续和解方案的时间与现值比较质疑萨克勒家族承担了更大经济牺牲，并区分州政府异议与最高法院挑战对不同债权人的影响。俄勒冈和华盛顿毒品非刑事化研究摘要报告逮捕减少但药物过量死亡增加，摘要未展示识别方法，不能仅凭标题外推。BoE两篇文章分别把能源转型影响通胀的渠道分解为支出构成、天然气与电价关系及电网投入压力，并把气候经济损失估计的主要不确定性定位于损害函数。横跨这些材料的共同结论是：规则目标之外，实施能力、控制结构与测量方法往往决定政策和治理效果。

### ECGI（3 篇）

#### The Political Consequences of Stakeholder Governance
**作者**：Doron Y. Levit, Markus Parlasca, Paul Voss
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：We study the political economy of stakeholder governance and corporate lobbying. Lobbying shifts policy toward outcomes that increase the firm's profitability, but it also provokes a backlash from advocacy groups, which push policy back toward the median citizen's preferred outcome. When lobbying is opaque, this backlash generates a ratchet effect: lobbying and backlash escalate, fueling political polarization and instability with little or no policy gain for the firm. To buy quiet, firms adopt stakeholder governance, delegating lobbying to a manager who places less weight on profits and more on citizens' welfare and therefore lobbies less. When the conflict between the firm and society is small, stakeholder governance leads to more socially responsible policies without sacrificing the firm's profits. Paradoxically, when the conflict is large, delegating to a more socially minded manager benefits the firm at society's expense, producing more extreme, socially harmful policies, even when the delegation creates no new agency problems. Our results suggest that stakeholder governance can have unintended political consequences and offer novel implications for corporate governance and lobbying regulation.
**关键词**：Purpose; Transparency; Comparative governance; Responsible investment; Agency theory; Corporate form; AI; Voting; CSR; Incentives; Labour; Regulatory competition; Shareholder rights
**主题**：公司治理
**原文链接**：https://www.ecgi.global/publications/working-papers/the-political-consequences-of-stakeholder-governance

#### The CEO Overhang Effect
**作者**：Maria Cecilia Bustamante, Jakub Hajda, Boris Nikolov
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：We document that CEOs reduce investment, increase net leverage, and expand net share repurchases over their tenure. To explain these patterns, we develop a dynamic model of a firm run by a sequence of CEOs. Each CEO has a finite tenure and chooses investment, financing, and payout to maximize her utility over wealth under a contract that combines a base salary with equity-based pay. The model identifies a new mechanism, the CEO overhang effect, which arises because the CEO's horizon is truncated and future CEOs are also granted equity. As a result, over her tenure a growing share of the returns to the firm's capital and of its costs of debt shifts from current shareholders, including the CEO, to future CEOs. The incumbent CEO therefore underinvests and levers up. She also increasingly repurchases shares, because a larger stake becomes more valuable to her as turnover approaches. Calibrating the model, we infer that the resulting agency cost amounts to 6% of shareholder value.
**关键词**：CEO & succession; Incentives; Agency theory; Labour; Board effectiveness; Family firms; Human capital; Bankruptcy
**主题**：公司治理
**原文链接**：https://www.ecgi.global/publications/working-papers/the-ceo-overhang-effect

A dataset compiled by Yuliya Guseva and co-authors counts only about 120 challenges between 1934 and 2024. More than half came in the last 15 years, with a further spike during the tenure of Chair Gary Gensler. She turned the usual question around. The puzzle is not only why there is so much litigation now, but why there was so little before. That puzzle matters because the SEC is a very powerful regulator. Many of its techniques elide traditional APA accountability mechanisms, turn on agency discretion, and allow micromanagement of market practices that is not obvious from the statute. Two familiar explanations for the recent wave are a broad crusade against the administrative state or a reaction to an overly aggressive SEC under the Biden administration. Both may be part of the story, but neither is complete.

### OBLB（2 篇）

#### Purdue Pharma, the Sackler Family and the Costs of Delay
**作者**：Robert K Rasmussen
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：The bankruptcy of Purdue Pharma generated widespread controversy over whether the Sackler family could obtain protection from civil liability without filing for bankruptcy themselves. Much of the public discussion focused on the steadily increasing amounts the Sacklers agreed to contribute to Purdue’s creditors. Their initial commitment of $3 billion ultimately became a settlement requiring at least $6.5 billion. The assumption that these increases represented substantially greater economic sacrifices by the Sacklers cannot withstand scrutiny. Examining the terms and timing of successive agreements, it turns out that the family’s ultimate financial commitment was roughly equivalent, in present-value terms, to what it agreed to before Purdue filed for bankruptcy in September 2019. Indeed, depending on the appropriate discount rate, the Sacklers may have benefited financially from the prolonged litigation. Moreover, they face little risk that they will face additional civil liability. The additional litigation had differing consequences for creditors. Although objections by certain state governments produced meaningful increases in their recoveries, the US Trustee’s successful Supreme Court challenge to nonconsensual third-party releases likely reduced the economic value available to the personal injury claimants.

#### The Illusion of Justice: Dispute Resolution on Polymarket
**作者**：Anna-Sophie Hochgürtel, Horst Eidenmüller
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：Prediction markets are experiencing remarkable growth. Yet their success depends on a crucial question: who decides whether a prediction has come true? Polymarket, a leading prediction market, relies on decentralized voting to resolve disputed outcomes. We argue that this mechanism suffers from fundamental due process deficiencies, particularly a lack of independence of decision-makers whose financial interests may conflict with impartial adjudication. We examine these deficiencies and consider two reforms: independent human adjudication and AI-based dispute resolution. Prediction markets are booming. Platforms such as Polymarket and Kalshi allow participants to trade contracts whose payouts depend on the outcomes of specified future events. These range from election results and geopolitical developments to economic indicators and corporate decisions. By aggregating participants’ expectations, market prices can provide information about the likelihood of these events. The commercial potential is attracting considerable investor interest. In July 2026, The Economist 1843 reported that Kalshi and Polymarket were seeking funding at valuations of approximately $40 billion and $15 billion, respectively. But who determines whether the predicted event has actually occurred? And what happens when the answer is disputed? Prediction-market contracts may contain ambiguities that become apparent only when the market must be resolved.
