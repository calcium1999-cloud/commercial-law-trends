### OBLB（1 篇）

#### The Reliance Gap in AI Corporate Governance
**作者**：Wen Yi Leong
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：Leading AI developers have built their safety commitments out of corporate law, such as in the form of public benefit corporations, controlling foundations, special share classes, trusts and published safety frameworks. As it turns out, each of these commitments can be lawfully loosened by those it was meant to constrain, to which few outsiders have the means to object. Yet regulators rely on these commitments when deciding whether to intervene. This post names the resulting shortfall the “reliance gap”, explains why corporate law addresses it inadequately, and proposes remedies such as consent rights and a one-way amendment ratchet which can be applied to any company whose public commitments the public relies upon. In October 2025, California’s Attorney General agreed not to object to OpenAI’s restructuring. Under the memorandum of understanding , the non-profit Foundation controls the new public benefit corporation insofar as it holds Class N stock, and “The decision whether to relinquish the Class N Common Stock, if ever, is in the control of the NFP Board” (para 2). A Safety and Security Committee may require mitigations “up to and including halting the release of models or AI systems” (para 11). Likewise, Anthropic’s Long-Term Benefit Trust gives trustees a special share class entitled to elect a growing part of the board. These are guardrail devices built from material designed for another job.
**关键词**：
**主题**：公司治理
**原文链接**：https://blogs.law.ox.ac.uk/oblb/blog-post/2026/11/reliance-gap-ai-corporate-governance
