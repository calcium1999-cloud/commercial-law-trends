#### Common Ownership around the World
**作者**：Miguel Antón, Florian Ederer, Mireia Giné, Guillermo Ramirez-Chiang, Kenneth Khoo
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：We study common ownership in 49 countries from 2005 to 2023, covering economies that account for 86% of global GDP. Common ownership is pervasive and rising around the world: average pairwise implied profit weights (κ) more than doubled in the median country over our sample period and more than tripled in the United States. Common ownership exhibits a steep size gradient—highest among the largest firms and rising fastest at the top of the firm size distribution—a pattern we observe consistently across all countries and regions. We show that the rise of common ownership stems not just from more institutional investment but from its increasing concentration among a few megaproviders of capital such as the Big Three (BlackRock, Vanguard, State Street). Even outside the United States, where non-Big Three investors remain important, the Big Three account for the largest share of the increase in common ownership in most countries. We also investigate how common ownership is related to legal, institutional, and market characteristics such as investor protection laws, competition laws, mandatory ESG disclosure, and labor market frictions across firms and countries. Our results are generally robust to replacing the one-share-one-vote assumption with control rights measured from dual-class share structures and ownership chains.
**关键词**：Common Ownership; Institutional Investors; Capital; Investment Decisions; ESG; Technology; Boards; Voting; Competition; M&A; Agency; Hedge Funds; Activism

### OBLB（2 篇）

#### Don’t Bet on Bankruptcy
**作者**：William Organek, Nizan Geslevich Packin
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：Prediction markets have exploded in popularity, transforming from academic curiosities into multibillion-dollar, consumer-facing platforms that facilitate trading on politics, sports, geopolitics, and even clinical-trial outcomes and Food and Drug Administration (FDA) approvals. To date, media, political, and judicial attention has largely been focused on the legal battles over whether prediction markets should be legal and if so who should regulate them, insider trading, market manipulation, and morally controversial markets. Yet amid this scrutiny, one question has received little scholarly attention: what happens if a prediction market fails? We address this question, the many complexities it raises, and the effect these questions have on the design of insolvency regimes in our new article , Don’t Bet on Bankruptcy: Prediction Markets and the Limits of Insolvency Law , forthcoming in the American University Law Review. Prediction markets, and the event contracts sold on them, include features of gambling, derivatives exchanges, and platform marketplaces. They also have features distinct from any of these treatments, as recent controversies surrounding bets on war and politics, manipulation of informational inputs, and potential insider trading demonstrate.

#### GPU and Connected Data Centre Equipment Financing: An Indian Legal Perspective
**作者**：Aditya Sharma
**Affiliations**：
**摘要**：As the demand for AI compute grows, financing the infrastructure behind it is emerging as a legal and commercial question. Nvidia’s recent move to establish compute financing platforms, alongside a proposed regulatory framework in India, signals an emerging financing model. India’s International Financial Services Centres Authority (‘IFSCA’) has proposed recognising the leasing of Graphics Processing Units (‘GPUs’) and connected data centre equipment as a financial product. GPUs are specialised processors well suited to performing the intensive processing required for AI and other advanced technologies. The IFSCA is a statutory authority that regulates (a) financial products and services offered in, and (b) financial institutions operating in, India’s International Financial Centres. Currently, India only has one recognized International Financial Centre, i.e., the Gujarat International Finance Tec-City (commonly known as ‘GIFT City’). This post considers the legal and commercial issues that could shape the development of the leasing of GPUs and connected data centre equipment as a financial product. In August 2026, Nvidia announced partnerships with six global financial institutions to establish compute financing platforms at global scale. Nvidia stated that the platforms will ‘broaden access to AI infrastructure’ and help customers access scarce compute at scale.
